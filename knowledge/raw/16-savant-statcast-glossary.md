# Statcast Metrics Context

How much do our selected underlying bat tracking and quality of contact matter to tangible statistics and performance at the plate? This page allows you to compare those outcomes.

## Metric Definitions

- **Barrel:** For a batted ball, the ideal combination of exit velocity and launch angle. Minimum xBA of .500 and xSLG of 1.500.
- **Blast:** A swing that is an ideal combination of squared-up and with strong bat speed. Defined as percent squared up * 100 + bat speed >= 164.
- **Fast Swing:** A swing that has 75 mph or greater bat speed.
- **Hard-Hit:** A batted ball hit with 95 mph or greater exit velocity.
- **Ideal Attack Angle:** A batted ball hit with an attack angle between 5 degrees and 20 degrees.
- **Launch Angle Sweet-Spot:** A batted ball hit with launch angle between 8 degrees and 32 degrees.
- **Squared-Up:** A swing where at least 80% of the potential exit velocity (based on swing speed and pitch speed) is converted into actual exit velocity.

**Note:** Barrels, Hard-Hit Rate and Launch Angle Sweet-Spot % metrics have data from 2016-present. Blasts, Fast Swing, Ideal Attack Angle and Squared-Up metrics have data from 2023-present (2023 bat tracking data is partial).

## Key Takeaways

1. **Barrels are the most impactful contact type** — .684 BA and 2.236 SLG vs. .292/.368 for non-barrels
2. **Hard-hit balls (.477 BA) vastly outperform soft contact (.224 BA)** — exit velocity is a dominant predictor
3. **Blasts (bat tracking metric) correlate strongly with production** — .546 BA for blasts vs .249 for non-blasts
4. **Launch angle sweet spot (8-32 degrees)** yields .584 BA vs .195 outside that range
5. **Squared-up contact** (.377 BA) outperforms poorly-struck balls (.242 BA) by 135 points
